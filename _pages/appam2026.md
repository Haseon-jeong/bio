**Did COVID-19 Redraw the Geography of Korea's National R&D Collaboration?**

Haseon Jeong · Center for Data Analysis Innovation, Office of Innovation Strategy, Korea Institute of S&T Evaluation and Planning (KISTEP)
APPAM 2026 · Poster Session

Short answer
======
Not overnight. New partnerships reached **farther after 2020, gradually**, and the reach-out
was led by **universities and SMEs**, not large firms.

Data and design
======
- Korea's national R&D project micro-data (NTIS), 2015–2024: 131,813 newly formed
  lead–partner ties across 65,602 projects
- Outcome: **route (road) distance** between lead and partner institutions
- Unit: newly formed ties. Only 5.7% of multi-year ties ever change distance, so a pandemic
  effect can only appear when partners are chosen.
- Interrupted time series and event study (ref. 2019), controls for institution type,
  research stage, budget and private funding; standard errors clustered by project

Findings
======
1. **No 2020 shock; the trend bent upward afterward.** The 2020 level shift was slightly
   negative, but the yearly slope rose by 0.073 log points after 2020. New ties were about
   19% farther in 2024 than in 2019.
2. **Universities and SMEs reached out; large firms and government research institutes pulled in.**
   Applied and development research expanded; basic research did not change.
3. **Structure still binds.** SMEs remain the most local, distance falls from basic to
   development research, bigger budgets reach farther, and private co-funding keeps partners close.

Pre-trends are not flat, so these results describe a *pandemic-era acceleration*, not a causal effect.
